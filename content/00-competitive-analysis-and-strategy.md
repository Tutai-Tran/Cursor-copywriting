## 2. Old Site Analysis: m-asia.nl

### Structure
- 4 pages: Home, About Us, Consultancy, Contact
- Simple, professional design
- Key CTA: "Catalog" button linking to shop.m-asia.nl

### Content Strengths
- Clear positioning: "Your eyes in China"
- Direct, confidence-building tone
- Strong testimonial section (5 named testimonials)
- Concrete numbers (60+ products, 100% dedication, 21+ years)
- Transparent about the founder's personal background
- Bilingual support (English/Dutch via GTranslate)

### Content Weaknesses
- Very thin content — few pages with limited depth
- No dedicated service pages
- No sector-specific content
- No FAQ or educational content
- No blog or knowledge articles
- No structured SEO strategy (generic title tags)
- Single CTA (Catalog) — no lead generation form
- No schema markup beyond basic Yoast

### Key Positioning Elements to Preserve
- "Your eyes in China" tagline
- Personal, founder-led brand voice
- Testimonials from real clients (Alex, Lieke, Bart, Liesbeth, Mau)
- Emphasis on transparency ("you always have the last word")
- "We are there on the spot" factory presence messaging

### Tone of Voice
Professional but personal. Confident without being aggressive. Uses "we" language and directly addresses the client. Focuses on trust and control. No marketing fluff — statements are backed by practical explanations.

## 3. Competitor Analysis: coolenchina.com (Coolen China)

### Structure
- Comprehensive site: Home, 10+ service pages, About, Knowledge articles, Contact
- Dutch language (NL-focused, with EN/DE options)
- Modern, well-structured layout with video hero
- Strong service differentiation (3 sourcing tiers)

### Content Strengths
- **Service tiering:** Three distinct sourcing options (Complete, Building on your pre-selection, Trading) — makes it easy for visitors to self-select
- **Knowledge articles:** Compliance topics (GPSR, Chinese New Year planning) — excellent for SEO
- **Personal founder story:** Ties Coolen, 20+ years in China, lived there
- **Large team showcased:** 25+ team members displayed by name and role
- **USP bar:** "Knowledge and experience / Custom & relief / No commission / Reliable partner / Exclusivity in production / Own team in China"
- **Process visualisation:** Clear funnel from service selection to outcome
- **Calls to action:** Well-placed throughout — contact form, phone number, email all prominently displayed
- **Newsletter signup** in footer

### Content Weaknesses
- Some content is thin or repetitive across service pages
- Knowledge articles section is small (only ~5 articles)
- No English case studies (Dutch only)
- No FAQ sections on service pages
- No client testimonial section (unlike old M-Asia)

### Key Patterns to Adopt (Without Copying)
1. **Service tiering** — Coolen's 3-tier approach is effective. M-Asia should clearly differentiate between full sourcing, QC-only, and trading.
2. **Knowledge content** — Compliance articles (GPSR, EN71) are an immediate SEO opportunity that M-Asia should replicate.
3. **Founder visibility** — Personal founder story builds trust. M-Asia has this with Vinod Gopani — make it more prominent.
4. **Team visibility** — Display the full team with real names and roles (currently M-Asia has placeholder names).
5. **Process clarity** — Show clear steps with icons/numbers (M-Asia has this with the "How We Work" section — refine it).
6. **USP bar** — M-Asia has this ("Best product sourcing in NL / Personal approach / 4.0/5 on Google") — keep and enhance.

### Keyword Opportunities Coolen China Targets (that M-Asia Should Also Target)
- Sourcing China (NL and EN variants)
- Inkoop China / Purchasing China
- Kwaliteitscontrole China / Quality control China
- Contracten China / Contracts China
- Transport advies China / Transport advice China
- GPSR compliance
- Leveranciers China / Suppliers China

## 5. Tone of Voice Guidelines

### Summary
Professional, clear, and confident. Not aggressive, not overly friendly. The tone of a knowledgeable partner who respects the reader's intelligence.

### Do
- Use "we" (M-Asia) and "you" (the client) language
- Be specific — use numbers, timelines, process steps
- Address real concerns and objections directly
- Explain *why* things work, not just *what* M-Asia does
- Use short-to-medium sentences
- Lead with outcomes, follow with method

### Don't
- Use superlatives without evidence ("the best", "world-class", "unparalleled")
- Use vague marketing phrases ("synergy", "leverage", "holistic approach")
- Over-promise ("guaranteed results", "zero risk")
- Use excessive exclamation marks
- Write in passive voice when active is clearer
- Copy competitor language

### Sentence Examples

**Too vague:** "We provide excellent sourcing solutions tailored to your needs."
**Better:** "We shortlist three to five vetted factories per product, with comparable quotes and a clear recommendation."

**Too aggressive:** "Stop wasting money on unreliable Chinese suppliers!"
**Better:** "Many teams overpay because they cannot benchmark factory pricing against the market. We provide that benchmark."

**Too informal:** "Hit us up for a chat about your next project!"
**Better:** "Tell us about your product and timeline. We reply the same day."
